Where this data comes from

  • Oakland CrimeWatch: Live Socrata API from data.oaklandca.gov, dataset ID ppgh-7dqv, 90-day rolling window, no login required, block-level coordinates
  • San Leandro CityDash: Live Google Looker Studio dashboard at sanleandro.city911.citydata.ai, powered by LotaData, fed by CAD dispatch, updated every 60 minutes
  • Annual aggregate stats: FBI Uniform Crime Report and city PD year-end reporting

What this data is not

These are incidents reported to police. They are not a complete picture of crime. Property and vehicle crimes are commonly reported when insurance is involved. Sexual assault, harassment, and many scams are systematically underreported, often by an order of magnitude.

Block-level coordinates are deliberately fuzzy. A pin on the map represents the block, not the exact house or business.

How to read it together with community reports

The community map on the main page shows what neighbors are seeing now. The official feed shows what made it into police reports over the past 90 days. The gap between the two is the actual story. If a corridor has heavy community reporting and light official data, it likely means people are not bothering to file reports because they expect nothing will happen.

Paid vs free

Services like Citizen, NeighborhoodScout, and SpotCrime resell this same public data with a polished interface on top. You are paying for the presentation, not the data itself. Everything shown here is free, public, and government-sourced.

What this map shows

The corridor map documents incidents along the East 14th Street and International Boulevard corridor shared by San Leandro and Oakland. This stretch accounts for a disproportionate share of both cities' reported property and vehicle crime. The map makes visible what aggregate statistics mask: geographic concentration. Half the incidents in San Leandro cluster in roughly 20% of the city's area.

Data sources: San Leandro PD NIBRS annual reports and OPD CrimeWatch. Prostitution-related calls are systematically underreported in official SLPD data. This gap is documented in the map's methodology notes.

ABOUT THE REGISTRY

What this is, and what it is not

The premise

Police reports get filed and disappear into a database. Consumer complaints get mailed to agencies and disappear into a different one. Patterns visible to neighbors on the same block are invisible to the systems supposed to track them. This registry exists so the people closest to the problem can see what the people closest to the problem are seeing.

What gets logged

  • Property crime, vehicle crime, package theft
  • Scams and fraud targeting consumers
  • Contractor disputes, billing fraud, defective products
  • Harassment, stalking, repeated targeting

What this is not

Filing here does not file a police report. It does not file a complaint with the FTC, the California Attorney General, the Contractors State License Board, the BBB, or any other regulator. Use those channels for formal action. Use this registry to show the pattern.

Moderation

The community moderates itself. Any user-submitted report can be flagged. Reports flagged by three or more visitors are hidden from the public map. False or malicious reporting wastes everyone's time and undermines the registry's value.

Accessibility

This site is built to WCAG 2.1 AA standards. Use the high contrast and larger text toggles in the top bar. All interactive elements are keyboard-navigable. The map can be navigated by tabbing through filter buttons rather than dragging. Screen readers will announce report counts and new selections.

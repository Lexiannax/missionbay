Thank you to everyone who came out on Sunday afternoon, November 15, to talk about how Mission Bay gets ready for floods, earthquakes and other emergencies. Neighbors gathered around a big map of the park, shared what they know, and asked good questions.

## The main message

The City, the Alameda County Fire Department and the park owner can each help, and we want to work with all of them. But in a large emergency their crews will be busy across the whole city. For the first hours we will largely be on our own. A plan for 366 homes can't rest on a few volunteers. It needs every street.

## What neighbors pointed out on the map

- **Gates.** The park's north and south emergency vehicle gates are padlocked. Who owns them and how they open in an emergency are open questions for the park owner and the fire department.
- **Getting out.** Wicks Boulevard gets heavy traffic in both directions, and people on foot could end up walking among cars. Neighbors asked for traffic control in any evacuation plan.
- **Gathering places.** The Marina Community Center, on higher ground, was suggested as a gathering point. The clubhouse was suggested as a first meeting and first aid spot. Neither has been confirmed, and neighbors asked what happens if the clubhouse floods.
- **Low spots.** The railroad side of the park, where our one pump station sits, is where water collects first. See [Where the Water Might Go](/flood-map/) for a street-by-street estimate.
- **Neighbors who need help.** Residents who live alone, use wheelchairs or walkers, rely on power for medical equipment, don't read English, or have pets will need a plan made with them in mind.

Some things said at the meeting were one person's account and have not been checked, for example reports about flooding and water service in nearby neighborhoods. We will share what we can confirm.

## Questions we are taking to the agencies and the park owner

1. Who leads an evacuation, and who leads rescue?
2. Who owns the gates, and how do they open in an emergency?
3. How will residents who can't walk far get out? Could paratransit, AC Transit or school buses help?
4. Can the clubhouse and the community center hold everyone who needs shelter?
5. Where is the park's current emergency plan, and when is the next drill? California law has required parks to have a plan since 2010.
6. How much water can our pump move, and does it have backup power?

## Next steps

- **A visit from the City's Office of Emergency Services** was discussed for **Tuesday, November 17 at 2:00 PM**. It has not been confirmed in writing yet. **Place: to be confirmed.** We will post updates on the [Events page](/events/).
- **A written request** to park management and the park owner for the current emergency plan and drill schedule.
- **A count of households**, so we know who may need help. This will be voluntary and private.
- **Street captains** for each street. No one has signed up yet, so this is where you come in.
- **Questions to AC Transit and the regional transit agency** about buses and paratransit in an evacuation.

## How you can help

- **Be a street captain.** Keep a short list of neighbors on your street who may need a hand.
- **Share your skills.** If you have medical or first aid training, or equipment like a generator, let us know.
- **Help with languages.** We want this information in the languages our neighbors speak, especially Asian languages. Translators, people who can read aloud, and anyone who can introduce us to neighbors who don't read English are very welcome.
- **Keep drains clear.** Keep mulch, bark and leaves away from storm drains, especially before a storm.
- **Sign up for alerts** at [acalert.org](https://acalert.org).

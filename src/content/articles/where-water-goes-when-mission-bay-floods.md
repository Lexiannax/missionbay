The flood map handed out at the November 8 meeting is FEMA's real, official map. It is accurate, but it leaves out the two things that matter most for our 366 households: our single pump and rising bay levels.

FEMA's map shows ocean flood zones. It does not show what happens when rain falls and our one pump stops. Using a 2021 USGS laser survey of ground height, we estimated where rainwater would collect inside the park if the pump stopped for a whole storm.

**The short version.** Rain in our park runs downhill to the north end on the railroad side, around Santa Anita, Santa Ynez and Santa Thelma Way. On New Year's Eve 2022, Santa Ynez flooded by the pump house, roughly 8 to 14 inches deep. If the pump stops completely in a storm that big, streets there could get 2 to 3 feet. The south end, from Santa Rohn Way down to Santa Margarita, sits 2 to 3 feet higher and stays dry in a storm like that.

In the maps above, darker blue means deeper water. Depths are at street level. Homes on piers usually have floors 2 to 3 feet higher.

A 10-year storm drops about 3.3 inches of rain in a day. On December 31, 2022, Oakland set a one-day record of 4.75 inches, close to a 100-year storm for our location. The real 2022 flooding was shallower than the table because the pump kept at least partly running. Space ranges are taken from the map and are approximate.

## Why one pump is the weak spot

When the tide is up, rainwater can't drain out of the park on its own, so the pump does all the work. It is in the right place, at the lowest corner. The problem is that there is only one. If it loses power or breaks during a storm, nothing else moves the water.

Adding pumps at the higher corners would not help, because the water never gets there. What helps is a second or third pump at the low end, each able to run alone, plus backup power and a hookup for a rented portable pump.

## What you can do now

1. **Know your street.** If you live on Santa Anita, Santa Ynez, Santa Thelma Way, or the north half of Santa Monica or Santa Paula, plan for deep street water in big storms. Keep your car, medicines and documents where you can reach them if the streets fill.
2. **Ask management three questions.** How many gallons per minute can our pump move? Does it have backup power? Who do we call when it stops?
3. **Sign up for alerts.** Register for AC Alert, Alameda County's free emergency notices, at [acalert.org](https://acalert.org).

FEMA's official flood map for our area is accurate. It shows where the bay could flood. It does not show what happens inside the park when heavy rain falls and our one pump can't keep up. Neighbors used a 2021 USGS laser survey of ground height to estimate where rainwater would collect if the pump stopped for a whole storm.

This is a neighbor-made screening estimate, not an engineering study. We share it so every household can think about its own street and so we can plan together with the City, the fire department and the park owner.

## The short version

Rain in our park runs downhill to the north end on the railroad side, around Santa Anita, Santa Ynez and Santa Thelma Way. That is also where our one pump station sits, on Santa Ynez by the railroad. The south end, from Santa Rohn Way down to Santa Margarita, sits 2 to 3 feet higher and stays dry in these storms.

About 44 percent of the park's ground is lower than FEMA's 10-foot flood height for the west side of the park.

A 10-year storm drops about 3.3 inches of rain in a day. On December 31, 2022, Oakland set a one-day record of 4.75 inches. Space ranges are read from the map and are approximate.

## What we know and what we don't know yet

**What we know.** On December 31, 2022, Santa Ynez flooded near the pump house. A resident's photo shows standing water there, roughly 8 to 14 inches deep by our estimate from the photo, with a fuel truck at the pump house.

**What caused it is not settled.** One neighbor with emergency experience told the November 15 meeting that garden mulch and other debris washed into the drains and blocked them, and that the pump was not at fault. We have not found a written report that confirms or rules this out. Either way, keeping drains clear is something every household can help with.

**What we are still asking.** How much water the pump can move, whether it has backup power, and how often it is tested. Residents have asked the park owner for these records. We hope to share the answers here.

**About Heron Bay.** Some residents have said the Heron Bay neighborhood across the railroad is better protected than we are. City budget records show that a City-run Heron Bay maintenance district has paid for a levee, tide gates and marsh upkeep since 1996, and the 2021 survey shows Heron Bay's ground sits about a foot higher than ours on average. Residents also reported that Heron Bay has flooded from its sewers, which we have not confirmed. No study we know of compares the two neighborhoods in a real storm. Our ask is simple: a clear, written flood plan for Mission Bay too.

## Why we need the whole park

The City, the Alameda County Fire Department and the park owner can each help, and we want to work closely with all of them. But in a big storm their crews will be stretched across the whole city. For the first hours, Mission Bay will largely have to look after itself. That job can't fall on a few people. It works only if every street takes part.

## What you can do now

1. **Know your street.** If you live on Santa Anita, Santa Ynez, Santa Thelma Way, or the north half of Santa Monica or Santa Paula, plan for deep street water in big storms. Keep your car keys, medicines and papers where you can grab them.
2. **Keep drains clear.** Keep mulch, bark, leaves and yard waste away from gutters and storm drains, especially before a storm.
3. **Get alerts.** Sign up for AC Alert, Alameda County's free emergency notices, at [acalert.org](https://acalert.org).
4. **Know your neighbors.** Find out who on your street lives alone or depends on power for medical equipment, and agree to check on each other.
5. **Volunteer.** See below.

## Neighbors wanted

We are looking for residents who can give a little time:

- **Street captains**, one or two per street, to keep a simple list of who may need help.
- **Neighbors with medical training**, for first aid while help is on the way.
- **Translators and language helpers.** We want to share this information in the languages our neighbors speak, especially Asian languages. If you can translate, read a page aloud, or introduce us to neighbors who don't read English, please get in touch.
- **Anyone** who can make phone calls, knock on doors, or help with sign-ups.

To help, [contact the editor](/contact/) or speak to an MBRA board member.

## Where to check conditions

Neighbors suggested these at the November 15 meeting. Check them before and during a storm.

- **Tides for San Leandro:** [tide-forecast.com](https://www.tide-forecast.com/locations/San-Leandro/tides/latest), or the official NOAA predictions for the [Alameda tide gauge](https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/noaatidepredictions.html?id=9414750). Rain on top of a high tide is when our drains struggle most. Tide heights on these sites are measured from average low water, so they are not the same numbers as the ground heights on this page.
- **Weather forecast:** the [National Weather Service forecast for our park](https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php?lat=37.683&lon=-122.160), which also shows official flood watches and warnings, or [AccuWeather for San Leandro](https://www.accuweather.com/en/us/san-leandro/94577/weather-forecast/337231) and its phone app.
- **Flood maps and preparedness:** [FEMA's flood map for your address](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/search) and [FEMA.gov](https://www.fema.gov).
- **Emergency alerts:** [AC Alert](https://acalert.org), Alameda County's free alerts.

## Where this comes from

- FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, map panels 06001C0266H and 0267H, in effect since December 21, 2018. Check your address at [msc.fema.gov](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/search).
- USGS 3D Elevation Program laser survey of Alameda County, 2021.
- NOAA Atlas 14 rainfall totals for our location and NOAA tide gauge 9414750 at Alameda.
- City of San Leandro Shoreline Sea Level Rise Adaptation Master Plan, July 2026, and City budget records for the Heron Bay maintenance district.
- The estimate assumes the pump is off for the whole storm and that no water flows in from outside the park. Accounts from residents are reported as told and are not verified.

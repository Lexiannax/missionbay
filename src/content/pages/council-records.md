This page collects public records from San Leandro's City Council and staff reports. Each item links to the city's own record where one is available. Items marked "reported by" come from a secondary source and should be checked against the city record before you rely on them.

## Rent stabilization for apartments (Chapter 4-46)

- **December 15, 2025, special meeting.** Council directed staff to set the rent increase cap at the lower of 3% or 65% of the Consumer Price Index, with 2025 as the base year. Source: [Legistar file 25-528, staff report](https://sanleandro.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=7796333&GUID=805DC079-461B-41DD-B1D6-3FB682961A5E&Options=&Search=).
- **January 12, 2026, City Council.** First reading of the ordinance adding Chapter 4-46, Residential Rent Stabilization. Council approved it, and the city lists it as Ordinance 2026-001. Source: [Legistar meeting record](https://sanleandro.legistar.com/MeetingDetail.aspx?ID=1360838&GUID=6F21FCDE-55DF-4FCF-866F-CB6186BEA647&Options=&Search=).
- **February 2, 2026, second reading.** Reported by the East Bay Rental Housing Association as a 5 to 1 vote, with Mayor Juan Gonzalez voting no. The city's file does not yet show this vote in the copy checked for this page. Source: [EBRHA](https://www.ebrha.com/news/san-leandro-chooses-politics-over-evidence).
- **Who is covered.** The October 13, 2025 draft excluded mobile home spaces, which are regulated under the separate Mobilehome Space Rent Stabilization Ordinance. Source: [City of San Leandro work session presentation](https://www.sanleandro.org/DocumentCenter/View/14185/Powerpoint_101325_Draft-Rent-Stabilization-Ordinance?bidId=).

## Mobile home spaces (Chapter 4-39)

- The Mobilehome Space Rent Stabilization Ordinance is Title 4, Chapter 4-39. The city adopted it after two public readings in 2019, effective July 3, 2019. Source: [City of San Leandro](https://www.sanleandro.org/316/Mobile-Home-Space-Rent-Stabilization-Ord).
- Residents should confirm the current annual cap for their park with the city's Housing Services Division at 510-577-6006 before relying on any percentage.

## Senior residents

- **Exemptions requested.** At the stakeholder and community meetings, tenants asked the city to decide whether seniors, persons with disabilities or people with special needs should be exempt. Source: [October 13, 2025 work session](https://www.sanleandro.org/DocumentCenter/View/14185/Powerpoint_101325_Draft-Rent-Stabilization-Ordinance?bidId=).
- **Higher penalties.** The draft set greater civil penalties for violations against seniors, persons with disabilities or households with children. Source: same presentation.
- **Income-based limits rejected.** Staff did not include means testing because of legal risk. Source: same presentation.
- **Income-based senior protections proposed.** Reported by the landlord association as an alternative offered by Mayor Gonzalez before the February vote. Source: [EBRHA](https://www.ebrha.com/news/san-leandro-chooses-politics-over-evidence).
- **Meetings held at the Senior Community Center.** The September 9, 2025 community meeting had 49 attendees and the September 4 tenants meeting had 15. Source: [October 13, 2025 work session](https://www.sanleandro.org/DocumentCenter/View/14185/Powerpoint_101325_Draft-Rent-Stabilization-Ordinance?bidId=).
- **Upcoming.** The city lists a Rent Program open house on November 16, 2026, covering the rent registry, rent stabilization and resources for tenants and owners. Source: [City of San Leandro newsflash](http://www.sanleandro.org/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/476).

## Other meeting records

- **Public comment period.** The city received 19 written comments during the 60-day period from July 17 to September 17, 2025. Source: [October 13, 2025 work session](https://www.sanleandro.org/DocumentCenter/View/14185/Powerpoint_101325_Draft-Rent-Stabilization-Ordinance?bidId=).
- **Program cost options.** Staff estimated $1,390,000 for Option 1 (benchmark) and $1,606,000 for Option 2 (enhanced), with 4 and 5 full-time staff respectively. Staff said substantial compliance could take 3 to 5 years. Source: same presentation.
- **Eligible units.** Staff counted 7,693 units eligible for rent stabilization out of 32,840 total units. Source: same presentation.
